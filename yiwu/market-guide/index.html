A buyer's guide to Yiwu International Trade City: what each of the five districts and the new District 6 sell, how the floor layout works, and how to plan a sourcing day inside the market.

Quick answer

Yiwu International Trade City is organised into districts, and each district groups products by floor. Work out which district covers your category before you arrive, then navigate by floor rather than wandering. District 2 covers hardware, bags and appliances; District 3 stationery, cosmetics and garment accessories; District 4 socks, daily goods and footwear; District 5 imported goods, home textiles and auto supplies.

Location
Yiwu, Zhejiang Province
Structure
Districts 1–6, each with multiple floors of booths
District 6
Global Digital Trade Center, opened October 2025
Official online directory
Chinagoods — shop numbers match physical booths

Who this guide is for: First-time buyers heading to Yiwu, and buyers who visited once and left without finding the right floor.

What the market actually is

Yiwu International Trade City is not one building. It is a group of very large wholesale buildings — districts — each with several floors and tens of thousands of booths. The scale defeats the “walk around and see what’s there” approach. Buyers who arrive without a floor plan spend a day walking and leave with three business cards.

The efficient method is narrow: identify your category, find the district and floor, then work that floor properly.

Chinagoods, the official online platform, mirrors the physical market. Booths on the platform correspond to real shop numbers, which means you can build a list of shop numbers before you arrive. That single habit doubles the value of a first visit.

Two rules that save time:

  • A district is not a building you “pop into”. Districts are separated by distance; plan one district per half-day.
  • “Floor” beats “district” once you are inside. Work one floor systematically rather than moving between floors at random.

How to plan a first day

The evening before. Open Chinagoods, filter by your category, and write down 15–20 shop numbers with the district and floor. Note the products each booth claims to make.

Morning. Start with the district that matches your primary category. Walk the floor systematically — aisle by aisle — rather than crossing the building repeatedly. Collect catalogues and photograph the booth number with the product.

Lunch. Eat near the market rather than leaving the area; you will lose an hour to transport otherwise.

Afternoon. Return to the five or six booths that looked most credible and ask the questions you did not ask in the morning: MOQ per colour, sample cost, packaging options, lead time, payment terms.

Evening. Message every supplier you met on WeChat with a short summary of what you discussed. Memory decays fast; a same-day message also tests whether they respond.

Practical realities inside the market

  • Booths are showrooms, not warehouses. Stock usually ships from a nearby warehouse or factory.
  • Quotes are starting points. Prices for the same item vary by 20–40% between booths within a district.
  • MOQ is negotiable in mixed orders. If a booth wants 12 pieces per colour, ask whether six colours at six pieces works.
  • Sampling is normal. Expect to pay for samples; ask whether the cost is credited against a later order.
  • English varies. Larger booths usually have English-speaking staff; smaller ones may use translation apps. Have your requirements written down.

A booth is not a factory

This is the single most useful thing to understand before you walk in. Behind most booths sits one of four types of business, and the type decides what you can negotiate.

What you are talking to How to tell What it means for you
Factory direct-sales booth The card names a factory; they can describe the production process and offer a plant visit Best route to customisation and volume pricing; the sample may come straight from the plant
Trading company Very wide range across unrelated categories; cannot answer detailed process questions Useful for consolidating several items into one shipment; their margin is inside the price
Brand or regional agent Displays one brand’s range and quotes from a price list Little room to negotiate; useful when you want consistent quality of a known product
Export agent or buying office Asks what you need instead of showing a range Can source outside the market; you are paying for a service, not a product

Ask directly: “Are you the factory or a trading company? Can I visit the factory?” The answer matters less than how comfortably it is given. A trader who says “we are a trading company, our factory partner is in [city]” is easier to work with than a booth that claims to be a factory and cannot describe a single production step.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to cover all districts in one day. They are separated by distance; you will spend the day in transit.
  • Buying from the first booth that quotes. Compare at least three quotes for the same specification.
  • Not recording booth numbers. A photo of a product without a shop number is nearly useless the next day.
  • Assuming the market is open every day of your trip. Public holidays and booth-level closures break plans.
  • Skipping the online directory. It is free and it is the difference between a targeted day and a random one.

Frequently asked questions

Are the booths in Yiwu factories?

Most are not. A booth is a sales front for a factory, a trading company or an agent. Ask directly whether they are the manufacturer and whether you can visit the plant; the answer changes what you can negotiate on customisation, MOQ and price.

How many days do I need in Yiwu?

Two full days for one product family, three to four if you buy across several categories. One day is enough to confirm whether Yiwu is the right market for you, but not to negotiate and compare properly.

Can I buy small quantities?

Often yes. Many booths quote a minimum order quantity per style or per colour rather than per carton, and mixed orders are common. Always ask explicitly — MOQ varies enormously between booths selling similar products.

Is the market open every day?

Generally the market operates six days a week, but individual booths close on different days and the whole market closes for Chinese New Year. Confirm before booking travel, and never assume a specific booth will be open.

Do I need a Chinese phone number?

It helps enormously. Suppliers will want to add you on WeChat, and a local number makes verification and delivery contact easier. A data eSIM covers navigation but not necessarily app registration.
