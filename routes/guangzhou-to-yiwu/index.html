Guangzhou to Yiwu: Route & Transport Guide

Compare the realistic options for travelling from Guangzhou to Yiwu — direct flights, high-speed rail with a possible change, and overnight trains — with timing, cost logic and booking advice.

Quick answer

Flying is fastest door to door if a suitable Guangzhou–Yiwu service operates on your date. High-speed rail is more predictable and lets you work, but usually takes most of a day. Overnight trains save a hotel night but cost you a night of sleep — rarely worth it before a market day.

Distance
Roughly 970 km — south China to Zhejiang
Fastest
Direct flight, around 2 hours in the air
Most predictable
High-speed rail, often with a change at Hangzhou East
Booking
Passport required; book through official rail or airline channels

Who this guide is for: Buyers moving between the Canton Fair and Yiwu, or adding Yiwu to a Pearl River Delta trip.

The four realistic options

Option Typical duration Best for Watch out for
Direct flight CAN–YIW About 2 hours in the air; 5–6 hours door to door Time-poor buyers with a suitable service Limited frequencies; airport transfers at both ends
Flight via a hub 5–8 hours total When no direct service fits Connection risk, baggage through-checking
High-speed rail (direct or via Hangzhou East) Roughly 6–8 hours Predictable arrival; working on the move A full travel day; station transfers
Overnight sleeper 12 hours or more Saving a hotel night Poor sleep before a market day

All timings are approximate and must be checked against the current timetable. The section below gives the shape of the service — how many direct trains run, how long they take and roughly what they cost — rather than a live schedule, because times change with each timetable update.

What the timetable actually looks like

The corridor has more direct services than most guides suggest. As of November 2026, 12306 lists roughly five direct departures a day from Guangzhou South to Yiwu, plus slower conventional services from Guangzhou Baiyun.

Departure Arrival Duration Second class (indicative)
06:18 14:52 8h 34m ¥634
11:25 18:47 7h 22m ¥727
13:19 20:12 6h 53m ¥765
15:09 20:30 5h 21m ¥770

Return services mirror this pattern: the fastest leaves Yiwu around 09:22 and reaches Guangzhou South in about 5h 16m.

Three observations that matter more than the exact times:

  • The afternoon departure is the fastest. The 15:09 service takes about 5h 21m — roughly three hours less than the early morning one. If you can leave after lunch, do.
  • The distance is about 970 km, not the 1,100 km often quoted. That matters when you are estimating a travel day.
  • Second-class fares cluster around ¥630–770. Prices vary by train and booking window; check 12306 for your date.

Treat this as the shape of the service, not a schedule you can rely on next month. Book on 12306 with your passport and reconfirm before you travel.

Option 1: fly

Yiwu airport (YIW) is close to the city, which makes the arrival simple. The constraint is supply: frequencies between Guangzhou and Yiwu are modest compared with trunk routes.

If you fly:

  • Book a morning departure so a delay does not push you into the evening.
  • Check baggage allowance — sample-filled suitcases are heavy.
  • Allow 90 minutes from landing to hotel, including baggage and taxi.
  • Keep the flight refundable if your fair schedule might shift.

Option 2: high-speed rail

The most reliable way to arrive on a predictable schedule, and the option most buyers choose when they are not rushed.

The pattern:

  1. Guangzhou South to Hangzhou East (or a direct service to Yiwu if available).
  2. Hangzhou East to Yiwu, typically around an hour.
  3. Yiwu station to your hotel, 20–30 minutes by taxi.

Practical notes:

  • Your passport is your ticket. Book with the exact name and number you will travel on.
  • Arrive at the station 45–60 minutes early. Security and the size of Chinese rail stations are underestimated by first-time travellers.
  • Guangzhou South is huge — allow 15 minutes just to navigate from metro to departure hall.
  • Food: buy supplies before boarding; onboard options are limited.
  • Seat class: second class is fine for most buyers; first class if you need to work or sleep.

Option 3: overnight train

An overnight sleeper can look efficient on paper: leave in the evening, arrive in the morning, save a hotel night.

In practice, arriving at a wholesale market after a night in a soft sleeper is not the same as arriving after a hotel night. If you try it, book a soft sleeper rather than a hard sleeper, and do not schedule a negotiation-heavy first morning.

When to travel relative to the fair

The Canton Fair calendar creates two peaks:

  • Before a phase opens — flights and trains towards Guangzhou are busy; travelling away from Guangzhou is easier.
  • Between phases and after the fair — the Guangzhou–Yiwu corridor gets busy with buyers doing exactly what you are doing.

If you are combining both, the three-day gaps between phases (20–22 April and 28–30 April in the 141st session) are the natural travel windows. See the Canton Fair + Yiwu itinerary for how that sequencing works.

Cost logic, without invented numbers

We do not publish fares, because they change daily and vary by booking window. Instead, think in these terms:

  • Flight — higher headline cost, lower time cost.
  • High-speed rail — moderate cost, predictable time, more luggage hassle.
  • Overnight sleeper — lowest cash cost, highest recovery cost.
  • Hidden costs — airport transfers, station transfers, an extra hotel night, and meals on the move. These often exceed the fare difference.

Build the real comparison in your own currency using current prices, not averages from a blog.

Booking checklist

  • Passport name and number match the booking exactly
  • Departure station or airport confirmed — Guangzhou has multiple rail stations
  • Transfer time to the station included in your plan
  • Hotel in Yiwu booked for the arrival night
  • Market opening day confirmed
  • Power bank charged and offline maps downloaded

Common mistakes

  • Booking a late train and arriving after the market closes.
  • Assuming Guangzhou South and Guangzhou East are the same station. They are not, and the difference can cost you the train.
  • Flying into Hangzhou without checking the last train to Yiwu.
  • Carrying samples in a way that exceeds airline limits and paying excess baggage at the airport.
  • Travelling on the first day of a phase when every other buyer is doing the same.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do Guangzhou to Yiwu in one day?

Yes, by flight or by an early high-speed train. Both consume most of a day once transfers are included. Plan to arrive in Yiwu in the evening and start at the market the next morning.

Is there a direct high-speed train?

Direct services have operated on this corridor, but availability changes with the timetable. Check 12306 for your date; if there is no direct train, the common routing is via Hangzhou East.

Should I fly or take the train?

Fly if time is tight and a suitable service exists. Take the train if you want predictable arrival, more luggage allowance, and the ability to work. Long-haul high-speed rail in second class is comfortable but not quiet.

How much luggage can I bring on the train?

High-speed rail allows generous luggage in practice, but you carry it yourself and storage space is limited at busy times. If you are moving samples and catalogues, a flight with checked baggage may be easier.
