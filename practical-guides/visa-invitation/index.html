Visa & Invitation Letter for China

A practical starting point for China entry rules: business visa basics, when an invitation letter helps, how invitation letters are issued, and why the rules depend on your nationality and change often.

Quick answer

China's entry rules depend on your nationality, your purpose and the date you travel. Some passports qualify for visa-free entry or transit visa-free stays; others need a business (M) or tourist (L) visa. Check the Chinese embassy or consulate for your country and the National Immigration Administration site before booking anything.

Rule one
Requirements vary by nationality — there is no single rule for 'foreigners'
Common visa types
M (business), L (tourism), F (exchange and visits)
Invitation letter
Supports an application; it is not a visa
Verification
Check official sources close to your travel date

Who this guide is for: Buyers planning a first trip to China who need to know which entry route applies to them.

This page is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Chinese entry rules have changed repeatedly in recent years, and they differ by nationality. Always confirm with the Chinese embassy or consulate responsible for your place of residence, and with the National Immigration Administration, before you book travel.

Start with your passport, not with a blog

The most common mistake in China travel planning is treating “foreigners” as one group. They are not.

Three broad situations exist:

  1. Visa-free entry. Some nationalities can enter for a limited period without applying in advance, for permitted purposes.
  2. Transit visa-free. Some travellers passing through China to a third country can stay for a limited period without a visa, subject to route and port conditions.
  3. A visa is required. Business (M), tourism (L), exchange (F) or another category, applied for before travel.

Which applies to you depends on your nationality, your purpose, your port of entry, your length of stay and the date. Any article that gives a single answer is wrong for most of its readers.

Visa categories that matter for buyers

  • M — business visa. For commercial and trade activities: meetings, negotiations, supplier visits, trade fairs. Usually the right category for a sourcing trip.
  • L — tourist visa. For tourism. Not intended for business activity.
  • F — exchange and visits. For non-commercial visits, study tours and similar programmes.

Applying in the wrong category can cause problems at the border even if the visa was issued. State your purpose accurately.

The invitation letter, explained properly

An invitation letter is a document from a Chinese entity — a supplier, a fair organiser or a business partner — confirming who you are and why you are coming.

A useful letter includes:

  • Your full name, passport number and nationality
  • Your company and role
  • The inviting company’s name, address and contact details
  • The purpose of the visit
  • Intended dates and places to be visited
  • Who covers costs
  • A signature and company stamp

What it does not do:

  • Guarantee visa approval. The decision belongs to the consulate.
  • Replace other documents. Financial evidence, itinerary and insurance may still be required.
  • Work as a visa on arrival. It is supporting paperwork.

How to request one

  • From a supplier: ask your main supplier for a stamped invitation on company letterhead, with the details above. Most established exporters can provide this.
  • From a fair organiser: the Canton Fair and similar events provide invitation routes for registered buyers. Follow the official process rather than a paid intermediary.
  • From a hotel: some hotels issue accommodation confirmations, which are not the same as an invitation letter.

Do not pay a stranger for a “guaranteed” invitation. If an agent promises approval, that is a warning sign.

Documents typically requested

Requirements vary, but business visa applications commonly ask for:

  • Passport with sufficient validity and blank pages
  • Completed application form and photograph
  • Invitation letter from the Chinese company
  • Company registration or a letter from your employer
  • Travel itinerary and accommodation bookings
  • Financial evidence
  • Travel insurance, in some cases
  • Appointment confirmation, where the visa centre requires one

Check the exact list from your local visa centre — this page cannot substitute for it.

Timing

  • Apply as early as the consulate allows. Appointment availability varies.
  • Do not book non-refundable travel before your visa is issued, unless your entry route does not require one.
  • Leave a margin for additional document requests. Consulates sometimes ask for more evidence, which restarts the clock.

If you are visiting Hong Kong or Macau too

Hong Kong SAR and Macau SAR have their own immigration arrangements, separate from mainland China. A mainland visa does not automatically cover them, and vice versa.

If your itinerary includes a Hong Kong flight connection, check both sets of rules. Many buyers fly into Hong Kong and cross to Shenzhen; that route has its own documentation considerations.

At the border

  • Carry printed copies of your invitation, hotel bookings and return ticket.
  • Be ready to state your purpose and length of stay clearly.
  • Have your host’s address and phone number available.
  • Register your accommodation as required — hotels do this automatically for foreign guests.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming your nationality is visa-free because it was two years ago.
  • Applying for a tourist visa for a business trip.
  • Booking flights before checking whether a visa is needed.
  • Paying an agent for an invitation a supplier would provide free.
  • Forgetting that Hong Kong SAR and the mainland have separate entry rules.
  • Arriving with no printed documents because everything was on a phone that has no data yet.

  • — Transit visa-free eligibility and the routes that qualify.

Frequently asked questions

Do all foreign visitors need a visa for China?

No. A number of nationalities currently benefit from visa-free entry or transit visa-free arrangements, but the list changes. Check the official sources for your passport before assuming either way.

Is an invitation letter required?

It depends on the visa category and the consulate. Many business visa applications are strengthened by an invitation from the Chinese company or event organiser. An invitation is supporting evidence, not a guarantee of approval.

Can I attend the Canton Fair on a tourist visa?

Attending a trade fair is business activity. Some visitors enter under arrangements that cover business visits, but you should confirm the permitted activities for your specific entry type rather than assume.

How early should I apply?

Apply as early as your consulate allows, and not less than several weeks before travel. Processing times vary by location and season, and appointments can be scarce.
