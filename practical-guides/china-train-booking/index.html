Booking China High-Speed Trains as a Foreigner

How to buy China high-speed rail tickets with a foreign passport: the official 12306 route, what happens at the station, seat classes explained, and when a booking platform is worth the fee.

Quick answer

Book on the official 12306 platform with your passport details entered exactly as printed. Your passport is your ticket at the gate — there is usually no paper ticket. Arrive 45–60 minutes early, and never book a train within three hours of an international flight.

Official channel
12306 (China Railway) — app and website
ID used
Passport; name and number must match exactly
Station arrival
45–60 minutes before departure
Ticket format
Electronic — passport used at the gate

Who this guide is for: Buyers moving between Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Yiwu and other sourcing cities who have not booked Chinese rail before.

The short version

China’s high-speed rail is the most reliable way to move between sourcing cities. The system is efficient; the paperwork is not always obvious to a first-time foreign traveller. Three things decide whether it goes smoothly:

  1. Your name on the booking matches your passport exactly.
  2. You arrive at the right station, early enough.
  3. You know your seat class and carriage.

Everything else is detail.

Booking on 12306 (official)

The official platform is China Railway’s 12306, available as a website and an app, with an English interface.

The sequence:

  1. Register with your passport details. Use the English name exactly as printed, including or excluding middle names consistently.
  2. Search your route and date. Note the departure station — large cities have several.
  3. Choose a train and seat class. Services are labelled by type (G, D, C and slower Z/T/K trains); G and D are the high-speed ones.
  4. Enter passenger details matching the passport.
  5. Pay. This is the step where foreign cards sometimes fail.
  6. Save the confirmation — screenshot it, and keep the passport accessible.

Practical notes:

  • Sales windows change. Tickets are not usually on sale months ahead; check the current window inside the app rather than trusting an old article (including this one).
  • Popular routes at holiday periods sell out fast. If your travel coincides with a Chinese public holiday, book as soon as sales open.
  • One passport, one ticket per train. You cannot hold two seats on the same service under the same ID.

Where foreign buyers get stuck

Three steps account for most failed first attempts:

  1. Passport registration is not always instant. Registering with a non-Chinese passport can trigger a manual check that takes a day or more. Do not leave registration until the night before travel.
  2. Phone verification. Some international numbers do not receive the SMS code reliably. If yours does not, use an authorised booking platform instead of fighting the form.
  3. Name format. Surname and given name go in separate fields, exactly as printed on the passport. Entering them in the wrong order is a common cause of being stopped at the gate.

Payment is the fourth: foreign cards work on some channels and not others. Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before you try to pay, rather than discovering the problem at checkout.

What happens at the station

Security first. Bags go through a scanner; you pass a gate with your passport. This is not optional and cannot be skipped by arriving late.

Find your train on the departure board. Note the waiting area and the gate number. Gates typically open 15–20 minutes before departure and close a few minutes before.

At the gate, place your passport on the reader. If the booking is valid, the gate opens. If not, staff will direct you to a counter — usually because of a name mismatch.

On the platform, carriage numbers are marked on the ground. Stand at your carriage number rather than boarding at random; the train stops briefly.

Seat classes, honestly

Class What you get When it is worth it
Second class 3+2 seating, standard legroom Most journeys; short hops
First class 2+2, wider seats, quieter Long routes where you need to work
Business class Lie-flat or large recliner, premium service Rarely necessary for sourcing trips

Luggage is self-carried. Overhead racks fit cabin bags; larger cases go in racks at the carriage ends. There is no checked baggage on high-speed trains.

When to use a booking platform instead

The official channel is cheapest. An international booking platform is worth considering when:

  • your card will not work on 12306;
  • you want an English interface and support if something goes wrong;
  • you are booking a multi-leg itinerary and want one confirmation;
  • you need a cancellation handled without navigating a Chinese-language process.

The trade-off is a booking fee and, occasionally, a slightly different cancellation route. Read the platform’s own terms — they are not the railway’s terms.

Timing rules that prevent disasters

  • Never book a train within three hours of an international flight. Delays compound.
  • Do not change cities on the morning of a meeting.
  • Allow 45–60 minutes at the station. Guangzhou South and Shanghai Hongqiao are enormous.
  • Check the last metro train if you arrive late; stations are often far from hotels.

Common mistakes

  • Booking from the wrong station. Guangzhou South, Guangzhou East and Guangzhou Baiyun are different places.
  • A middle name on the passport but not on the booking (or the reverse).
  • Arriving 15 minutes before departure and missing gate closure.
  • Assuming a paper ticket is needed and queueing for one.
  • Carrying samples with batteries in checked luggage — batteries belong in the cabin, within airline limits.
  • Buying a ticket for the wrong date because the app defaults to today.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a paper ticket?

Usually not. Your passport is read at the gate and the booking is linked to it. Some travellers still print a receipt for expenses, but it is not required for travel.

Can I book with a foreign credit card?

The official platform accepts some international cards, but acceptance is inconsistent. Many foreign buyers book through an international platform instead, or ask a colleague in China to pay. Test your card before you rely on it.

Why was my booking rejected at the gate?

Almost always a mismatch between the passport and the booking: a missing middle name, a space in the wrong place, or an old passport number. Re-enter the details exactly as printed on the photo page.

Is first class worth it?

For a long working journey, yes — quieter and more space. For a 30-minute hop between Guangzhou and Shenzhen, second class is fine.
