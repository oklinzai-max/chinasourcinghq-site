What to prepare before a China sourcing trip: documents and paperwork, money and payment setup, connectivity, a sourcing kit that actually gets used, and a seven-day countdown.

Quick answer

Three categories decide whether the trip runs smoothly: documents (passport, entry eligibility, bookings, invitation), money and connectivity (a working payment app and data before you land), and a small sourcing kit (notebook, tape measure, power bank). Everything else you can buy there.

Non-negotiable
Passport + working mobile payment + data on arrival
Digital backup
Passport photo page, bookings, insurance stored offline
Power
220V, plug types A / C / I — bring a universal adapter
Download
Printable checklist (.docx) at the top of this page

Who this guide is for: Buyers preparing for a first or second China trip, and anyone who has arrived without a working payment app.

Download the checklist (.docx) Six sections plus a seven-day countdown. Print it and tick as you pack — no email required.

Start with the three that break trips

Most packing lists are too long and miss the point. On a sourcing trip, three things cause real problems when they are missing:

  1. Passport and entry eligibility. Without a valid passport and the right entry route, nothing else matters. Confirm the rules for your nationality rather than relying on what worked for someone else — see visa and invitation letters.
  2. A working payment method. In practice this means a mobile payment app with a card linked, set up before you fly. The payment and connectivity guide walks through it.
  3. Data on arrival. An eSIM installed and tested, or a local SIM decision made. Landing without a map is a small problem; landing without a way to message your hotel is a bigger one.

Everything else on this page is optimisation.

Documents

Carry physically:

  • Passport, valid well beyond your return date
  • Visa, or evidence of your visa-free eligibility if that applies
  • Printed hotel bookings for every night
  • Return or onward ticket
  • Travel insurance policy and the assistance phone number
  • Invitation letter or fair registration confirmation, if you have one
  • Two spare passport photos

Store offline (phone album + a cloud folder you can access without local data):

  • Passport photo page
  • All bookings
  • Insurance certificate
  • Supplier addresses and phone numbers
  • Company profile you will show at booths

Business paperwork: a one-page company profile printed 20–30 times, business cards, specification sheets, and your target supplier list with booth or shop numbers.

Money

  • Two cards from different networks, both enabled for overseas use
  • Bank notified of your travel dates
  • Mobile payment app installed, identity verified, card linked
  • A small amount of RMB for incidentals
  • Card issuer emergency numbers saved offline

The most common failure is not a lack of money — it is a card that the bank blocked because it had never been used abroad. A two-minute call before you leave prevents a two-hour problem.

Connectivity and apps

  • eSIM installed and tested, or a local SIM plan decided
  • WeChat installed, logged in and verified
  • Offline maps of each city downloaded
  • Translation app with the offline Chinese pack
  • Power bank, charged, within airline limits
  • Universal power adapter — China uses 220V with type A, C and I sockets

Do not assume your usual work apps behave normally on mainland networks. Decide in advance how you will reach colleagues.

The sourcing kit

Short list, all of it used:

  • Notebook and two pens
  • Tape measure
  • Power bank
  • Folder for catalogues and business cards
  • Phone storage cleared for photos
  • A few sample bags and a marker

Optional: a small digital scale if you compare product weights, and a laptop if you plan to draft quotations in the evening.

Clothing

  • Comfortable walking shoes — market days run to tens of thousands of steps on concrete
  • Layers, because exhibition halls are cold and streets are warm
  • One smart-casual outfit for factory or supplier meetings
  • Basic medication in original packaging

The seven-day countdown

Days out What to do
7 Confirm flights, hotel and badge. Re-check entry rules.
5 Print documents. Download offline maps. Install and test the eSIM.
3 Notify bank and card issuers. Finish the supplier target list.
2 Pack the sourcing kit. Charge the power bank. Confirm your first meeting.
1 Re-check flight status. Save the hotel address in Chinese characters.

At the airport

  • Batteries go in the cabin, not checked baggage, within your airline’s limits.
  • Samples in hand luggage should be identifiable and declared if asked.
  • Keep the passport on your body, not in a checked bag or a seat pocket.
  • Photograph your checked bag before handing it over; useful if it goes missing.

Common mistakes

  • Relying on a card that has never been used abroad.
  • Installing the eSIM at the airport and discovering it needs manual configuration.
  • Packing six outfits and no tape measure.
  • Forgetting the Chinese address of the hotel — taxi drivers may not read English.
  • Carrying all documents in one bag that could be lost.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to print documents?

Print the essentials: passport photo page, hotel bookings, return ticket, insurance, and any invitation letter. Phones die, networks fail, and a border officer may want paper. Keep a second copy in a different bag.

How much cash should I carry?

A small amount for incidentals is enough. Mobile payment covers most situations, but cash helps when a QR code fails or a small vendor prefers notes. Do not carry large sums.

What sourcing kit is actually useful?

A notebook and two pens, a tape measure, a power bank, and a folder for catalogues. A scale is optional. Everything else — bags, markers, adapters — is easy to buy locally.

Can I bring product samples into China?

Generally yes for personal reference, but declare them if asked and keep them clearly identifiable. If you are bringing exhibition materials or large quantities, check the customs rules for temporary import before you fly.
