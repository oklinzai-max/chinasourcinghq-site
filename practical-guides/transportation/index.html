Getting Between Cities in China

How to move between Chinese sourcing cities: booking high-speed rail with a passport, airport procedures, ride-hailing and metro payments, plus the buffer times that prevent missed connections.

Quick answer

High-speed rail is the default for city-to-city travel under about five hours; flying wins on long routes. Book rail with the exact passport details you will travel on, arrive 45–60 minutes early at stations, and always leave a buffer between a flight and a train.

Rail booking
Passport required; official channel is 12306
Station arrival
45–60 minutes before departure
Airport arrival
2 hours for domestic, 3 for international
Payment
Mobile payment or cash; cards are inconsistent

Who this guide is for: Buyers moving between sourcing cities who want to avoid the classic missed-connection mistakes.

Rail or air? A simple rule

  • Under ~4 hours by rail — take the train. City-centre to city-centre, no baggage carousel, predictable.
  • 4–6 hours — depends on schedules and whether you can work on the train.
  • Over 6 hours — compare a flight seriously, especially if the rail route requires a change.

The Pearl River Delta is a special case: Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Foshan and Zhongshan are close enough that rail or metro is almost always the right choice.

How long the main hops actually take

Route Mode Typical time Frequency
Guangzhou South → Shenzhen North High-speed rail About 30–40 minutes Every few minutes
Guangzhou South → Zhongshan North Intercity rail About 30–40 minutes Frequent
Guangzhou → Foshan Metro (Guangfo line) About 30–40 minutes Every few minutes
Guangzhou South → Hong Kong West Kowloon High-speed rail About 50 minutes Frequent
Guangzhou South → Yiwu High-speed rail, direct 5h 21m to 8h 34m About 5 a day

Two things this table makes obvious:

  • The Delta is a day-trip zone. Shenzhen, Foshan and Zhongshan can be visited and returned from in a day, which is why buyers base themselves in Guangzhou and fan out.
  • Yiwu is not a day trip. At five to eight hours, it is a separate leg of the trip, not an afternoon excursion.

Times are typical; confirm on 12306 for your date. Note that on the Yiwu corridor the afternoon departure is faster than the early one — see the Guangzhou to Yiwu guide.

Booking high-speed rail

Your passport is your ticket. The name and number must match exactly, including spacing. A mismatch can mean being refused at the gate.

Ways to book:

  • 12306 — the official China Railway platform. Cheapest, requires registration and a payment method that works.
  • Authorised booking services — convenient, small booking fee, English interface.
  • At the station — possible, but English-language counters are limited and popular trains sell out.

Seat classes:

  • Second class — fine for most journeys; 3+2 seating.
  • First class — 2+2, quieter, better for working.
  • Business class — expensive, rarely necessary.

Practical notes:

  • Sales windows open on a rolling basis; popular routes at holiday periods sell out quickly.
  • Choose your station carefully. Guangzhou has several: Guangzhou South (high-speed), Guangzhou East, Guangzhou Baiyun. They are not interchangeable.
  • Luggage is self-carried; overhead racks fit cabin bags, larger cases go at the carriage ends.

At the station

  1. Enter through security — bags scanned, passport checked.
  2. Find your train on the departure board, note the waiting area and gate.
  3. Gates typically open 15–20 minutes before departure and close a few minutes before.
  4. At the gate, place your passport on the reader; the gate opens if the booking is valid.
  5. Follow the carriage numbers on the platform — they are marked on the ground.

Do not cut it close. Security queues at Guangzhou South or Shanghai Hongqiao can take 20 minutes at peak times.

Flying domestically

  • Book with the passport you travel on, not a local ID.
  • Arrive 2 hours early for domestic flights; Chinese airports are large and check-in closes early.
  • Baggage allowance matters if you carry samples — buy allowance in advance rather than at the airport.
  • Lithium batteries must go in cabin baggage, with capacity limits. Check the airline’s rules before packing samples with batteries.
  • Delays are common in storm season. Build buffers around important appointments.

Metro, taxi and ride-hailing

Metro is cheap, fast and the best default in big cities. Payment options include single tickets, stored-value cards, and QR codes inside Chinese apps. International cards work inconsistently at ticket machines — set up mobile payment before you rely on it.

Taxis are metered and inexpensive by international standards. Use official ranks at airports and stations; avoid unlicensed drivers approaching you inside terminals.

Ride-hailing works well, but the apps are in Chinese and pickup points can be confusing in large complexes. Have your destination written in Chinese characters.

The buffer rules

Rule 1 — never book a train within 3 hours of an international flight. Delays compound.

Rule 2 — do not change cities on the morning of a meeting. Travel the evening before.

Rule 3 — allow 60–90 minutes from landing to hotel at major airports, more if you check baggage.

Rule 4 — last trains matter. Metro systems generally stop before midnight; check the last train time when planning a late dinner.

What to keep on your phone

  • Passport photo page (and a cloud copy)
  • Booking confirmations, screenshots as well as emails
  • Hotel address in Chinese characters
  • Offline maps of each city
  • A working payment app
  • Your supplier’s WeChat contact

Common mistakes

  • Booking at the wrong Guangzhou station.
  • Name mismatch between passport and ticket.
  • Arriving 20 minutes before departure and missing the gate closure.
  • Carrying samples with batteries in checked baggage.
  • Assuming card payment works everywhere.
  • Scheduling a factory visit immediately after a flight.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a paper ticket for high-speed rail?

Usually not — your passport functions as the ticket at the gate. Keep the passport accessible, and collect a paper receipt only if you need one for expenses.

Can I book train tickets abroad?

Yes, through the official 12306 platform or an authorised booking service. Booking directly is cheaper but requires a working payment method and passport registration.

How early should I arrive at a Chinese rail station?

45–60 minutes. Stations are large, security is mandatory and queues at busy times are long. Add extra time if you are unfamiliar with the station.

Are domestic flights reliable?

Generally yes, but delays happen, especially in summer storm season. Avoid same-day flight-to-international connections unless the airline protects the connection.
