How to prepare for your first Canton Fair in Guangzhou: what to bring, how to build a target list, how to work the halls efficiently, and what to verify on the official site before you book.

Quick answer

The Canton Fair (China Import and Export Fair) runs twice a year in Guangzhou, in three phases of five days each. Pick the phase that matches your product category, register for a buyer badge through the official site before you travel, and treat the fair as a shortlisting tool — not a place to finalise orders on the first day.

Who this guide is for: First-time international buyers, and experienced buyers who want a repeatable process for working the fair without wasting days.

What the Canton Fair actually is

The Canton Fair — officially the China Import and Export Fair — is a trade exhibition, not a wholesale market. That distinction matters, because it changes how you should behave there. You will not walk out with stock. You will walk out with supplier contacts, catalogue numbers, quoted prices, and a much clearer idea of what your product really costs.

It has run twice a year since 1957, hosted in Guangzhou and organised under China’s Ministry of Commerce and the Guangdong provincial government. Each edition is split into three phases, and each phase covers a different set of product sections.

For a buyer, the fair is most useful for three things:

  • Comparing suppliers quickly. Dozens of factories for the same product in one building is unusual anywhere in the world.
  • Seeing product direction. New finishes, materials and packaging appear here before they reach wholesale markets.
  • Building a shortlist for factory visits. A conversation at a booth is a screening step, not due diligence.

The three phases: which one do you attend?

Dates below are from the official 141st session notice. Product ranges are summarised; the official site publishes the full section list.

Phase 3 1–5 May 2027 Toys, baby and children’s products, apparel, underwear, sportswear, fur and down, fashion accessories, fabrics, shoes, luggage and bags, home textiles, carpets, office supplies, health and medical devices, food, sports and leisure, personal care, bathroom products, pet products

A few practical consequences:

  • Electronics buyers go early. Phase 1 is the shortest gap from the fair opening, so flights and hotels around 14–20 April are the tightest of the whole cycle.
  • Homeware and furniture buyers benefit from the gap between phases. Phase 2 starts on 23 April, which leaves three days you can use for factory visits in Foshan or Zhongshan instead of waiting in Guangzhou.
  • Apparel, bags and gifts buyers attend Phase 3, which runs into the May public holiday period — book accommodation earlier than you think you need to.

If your product range straddles phases, choose the phase where most of your spend sits, then use the days between phases for the rest.

Which area and hall for your product

The complex is split into four areas — A, B, C and D — and each product section sits in specific numbered halls. A hall number reads as building.floor: 1.1 is building 1 ground floor, 14.4 is building 14 fourth floor.

Knowing the number before you arrive turns a 30-minute search into a 5-minute walk. The tables below use the 140th session (October 2026) official layout, which is the best available guide to the 141st — but allocations do change between sessions, so check the official map before you fly.

Reading the numbers

  • Y annexes are extension stands, often in corridors and side halls. The official map marks them separately; they are easy to walk past.
  • 0 floors are outdoor areas. Construction machinery, agricultural machinery and vehicles sit outside the main buildings. Buyers regularly miss them by searching indoors.
  • Gates 7 and 8 serve areas B and D most directly. If your target halls are there, enter by the nearest gate rather than walking from the main entrance.
  • 21.2 (Friendship Hall) is used for special zones such as service robots and fashion jewellery, not a regular product hall.

One day or three days

One day: stay inside a single area. Phase 1 electronics buyers can spend the whole day in Area A; Phase 2 homeware buyers can do Area A plus the furniture halls in B; Phase 3 apparel buyers can stay in Area A. Crossing between distant areas consumes the day.

Three days: Day 1 breadth in your main area, Day 2 depth with 8–12 shortlisted suppliers, Day 3 comparison and written quotations. That is the rhythm described in “How to work the halls” below.

Before you fly: the preparation that decides your trip

Most disappointing first trips fail in preparation, not at the fair.

1. Register for your buyer badge. Use the official channels, not a third-party agent, and do it weeks before you travel. Requirements differ by buyer type and nationality. See the buyer registration guide for the general sequence.

2. Build a target list before the halls. The official fair catalogue and exhibitor lists are searchable in advance. Aim for 15–25 target exhibitors with booth numbers, sorted by hall. Walking 30,000 steps without a list is the single most common mistake.

3. Prepare a one-page company profile. Product line, target market, typical order volume, compliance requirements (CE, FCC, RoHS, FDA, REACH — whichever apply), and a clear statement that you are a buyer, not a browsing visitor. Print 30 copies.

4. Decide your numbers in advance. Your target FOB price, acceptable MOQ, and the certification you cannot compromise on. Suppliers read hesitation. Having numbers written down keeps conversations short and useful.

5. Sort out payments and connectivity before arrival. International cards do not always work in every context in China, and your usual apps may not behave normally. Read payment and connectivity and set up a data solution before you land.

6. Book accommodation early and near a metro station. Fair-period room rates in Guangzhou rise sharply. The accommodation guide explains which areas trade price against commute.

How to work the halls without wasting days

A repeatable rhythm for a three-day visit:

Day 1 — breadth. Walk your main product section, collect catalogues, note booth numbers that look credible, and ask three questions at every stop: what is your MOQ, what is your FOB price at that MOQ, and which certifications do you already hold. Do not negotiate yet.

Day 2 — depth. Return to the 8–12 most credible suppliers. Ask about tooling costs, packaging options, lead times for a repeat order, and payment terms. Photograph the product, the packaging and the business card together so you can match them later.

Day 3 — comparison and follow-up. Revisit two or three finalists, request a quotation in writing with the exact specification, and confirm who you are speaking to — sales representative or factory owner. Then leave the fair and send follow-up emails the same evening, while your notes are fresh.

Two habits that pay off:

  • Write on the back of every business card. Date, hall, booth, what you discussed, and one honest impression. Unlabelled cards become useless within a week.
  • Record the quote, not just the price. A price without MOQ, packaging, lead time and Incoterm is not comparable to anything.

What the fair is not

The Canton Fair is a sourcing starting point. It is not:

  • A quality guarantee. Exhibitors are screened for participation, but a booth is not an audit.
  • A price floor. Factory-direct quotes from a trade fair are often beaten by a supplier you have already vetted, especially for simple commodity items.
  • A substitute for a factory visit. If the order value justifies it, visit the factory. If it does not, order a paid sample first.
  • A place to sign a big contract on day one. Use it to shortlist; negotiate afterwards with comparable quotes in hand.

Combining the fair with Yiwu

Many buyers pair the fair with a Yiwu trip. The logic: the fair covers factories and export-oriented suppliers, while Yiwu covers small commodities, low-MOQ mixed orders and an enormous range of consumer goods under one roof.

The most common sequence is Canton Fair first, then a high-speed train to Yiwu — roughly half a day of travel. Our Canton Fair + Yiwu itinerary sets out a realistic version, and the Guangzhou to Yiwu route guide compares the transport options.

Common mistakes

  • Arriving without a badge or with an unfinished registration. Fixing it onsite costs hours you could spend in the halls.
  • Booking a hotel because it is cheap. A 40-minute commute each way costs you two hours a day during the busiest week of the year.
  • Trying to cover all three phases in one trip. Each phase is a different industry. Pick one, do it well.
  • Negotiating before you understand the specification. Price without spec is a number, not an offer.
  • Leaving without written quotations. Verbal prices evaporate. Ask for the quote by email before you leave the booth.
  • Assuming a booth means a factory. Many exhibitors are trading companies. That is not automatically bad — but know which one you are talking to.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I need at the Canton Fair?

Two to three full days in the phase that matches your products is a realistic first visit. One day is enough to walk a single product section superficially; three days lets you revisit shortlisted suppliers and compare quotes properly.

Can I visit more than one phase?

Yes, if your buyer badge covers the session you can attend multiple phases, but each phase covers different product categories. Most buyers attend one phase and use the days between phases for factory visits or a Yiwu trip.

Do I need an invitation letter to register?

Registration routes and document requirements change. Check the buyer registration page on the official Canton Fair site for your nationality and buyer type before booking flights. This guide does not replace the official requirements.

Is the Canton Fair only for large buyers?

No. Exhibitors set their own minimum order quantities, and many will quote small trial orders. That said, the fair is a meeting and shortlisting environment — the strongest negotiations usually happen after the fair, not on the show floor.
