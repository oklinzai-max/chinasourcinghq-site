China Sourcing: Cities, Markets and Routes

Choose the right Chinese sourcing city for your products, understand what each city is good at, and plan a route with realistic day counts.

Good to know: City specialisms are broad guidance, not a rule. Verify the specific product cluster and supplier before committing to a trip.

There is no single best city for sourcing in China. The right answer starts with the product, then narrows to the city and cluster that actually makes it.

Browse by city

Each city guide follows the same structure: what it is good at, where the suppliers cluster, and what a realistic visit looks like.

Industrial clusters

Suppliers concentrate around the product, not around the city centre. Shenzhen's electronics, Zhongshan's lighting and Foshan's furniture each have their own districts, so the time saved by visiting several factories in one area is real.

City guide Guangzhou: Markets & Sourcing Guide A sourcing guide to Guangzhou beyond the Canton Fair: named wholesale markets by category, which metro station serves each, opening patterns, and how to choose between the fair and the markets. City guide Shenzhen: Electronics Sourcing Guide How to source electronics in Shenzhen: which Huaqiangbei building sells what, a two-hour first loop, when you need a contract manufacturer instead, and how to prepare specifications and certification questions. City guide Zhongshan: Lighting Sourcing Guide How to source lighting in Zhongshan's Guzhen lighting cluster: what to compare, which technical details decide your landed cost, and how to prepare certification and voltage requirements. City guide Foshan: Furniture & Building Materials Sourcing A buyer's guide to sourcing furniture, ceramics and building materials in Foshan: how Lecong works, what to compare on large goods, and how freight and damage shape your landed cost.

Supplier selection and verification

A quotation is not a supplier. Put every quote on the same basis — same quantity, Incoterm, currency and packaging — before comparing numbers, then score the non-price answers in the same table.

Tool · by email Supplier comparison workbook Score quotes against the same criteria; sample tracking is a sheet inside the same workbook. Guide Business communication with suppliers What to ask in writing, and how to keep a thread you can refer back to.

Sourcing process

A workable sequence: shortlist cities by product, shortlist suppliers in those clusters, compare quotes on equal terms, request samples, then visit the factories that survived the first round.

Cost and logistics

The unit price is only one line. Freight, duty, inspection and small domestic costs change the number you actually pay per unit — and they are easy to leave out of a first comparison.
