Foshan: Furniture & Building Materials Sourcing

A buyer's guide to sourcing furniture, ceramics and building materials in Foshan: how Lecong works, what to compare on large goods, and how freight and damage shape your landed cost.

Quick answer

Foshan concentrates furniture showrooms in Lecong and ceramics and building materials in Nanzhuang and Shiwan. Unlike small commodities, the price you negotiate is only half the decision — carton dimensions, loading quantities, damage risk and freight terms determine your real cost.

Guangdong

Who this guide is for: Furniture importers, interior and project buyers, and distributors of ceramics and building materials.

Two clusters, two different visits

Foshan is not one sourcing destination. It is at least two:

  • Lecong (Shunde District) — furniture showrooms at enormous scale, from mass-market to higher-end.
  • Nanzhuang and Shiwan areas — ceramics, tiles, sanitaryware and related building materials.

They are far enough apart that combining them in one day means spending much of it in a car. Plan separate half-days or full days.

The showrooms, and who each one is for

Lecong is not one market but a strip of showroom buildings along the 121 provincial road, reported at more than 180 buildings and roughly 4 million square metres of display space. You cannot “do Lecong” in a day. These are the buildings buyers actually use:

Showroom Positioning Go here if…
Louvre International Furniture Exhibition Centre (罗浮宫) The benchmark: 2,000+ brands and around 20 themed halls, also a national 4A-level tourism site You want to see current design direction, high-end and imported lines, or fit out a whole project
Sunlink North (顺联家居汇 北区) Mid-to-high-end retail plus factory-direct showrooms, next to Louvre You are buying for a home or a premium project and want factory showrooms with real interiors
Sunlink South (顺联家居汇 南区) The wholesale base: sofas, soft furniture, hotel and project furniture You are a dealer or project buyer; identical models are reported 20–40% cheaper than North, with much wider quality variation
Royal Furniture (皇朝家私) Four buildings split by tier: Royal and Noble (mid-high), Modern and Value (mid-low) You want the same supplier at two price points
Nanhua, Dongheng and neighbours Wholesale-first, price-led You are filling a container and can judge quality yourself
Red Star Macalline Lecong A national chain’s only wholesale-format store You want branded lines at wholesale terms
Lecong International Home Exhibition Centre Two blocks: soft furniture, bedroom, living, hotel and outdoor on lower floors; decor, Chinese and classical furniture, office and European lines above You want many categories under one roof

Two nearby towns change what Lecong means:

  • Longjiang (龙江) — the factory side. If you need customisation, a new frame or a private-label programme, this is where production actually sits.
  • Jiujiang (九江) — original design studios, for buyers competing on design rather than price.

A practical rule: Lecong for models and quotes, Longjiang for factories, Jiujiang for original design. Buyers who only visit Lecong often end up paying showroom margins for a product made 20 minutes away.

One day or three days

One day: Louvre in the morning to calibrate quality and price, Sunlink North or South in the afternoon depending on whether you are retail or wholesale. That is a realistic day.

Three days: Day 1 Louvre and Sunlink North for models and benchmarks. Day 2 Sunlink South, Nanhua and Dongheng to compare quotes on the same specification. Day 3 Longjiang factory visits for the two or three suppliers you shortlisted. This sequence stops you from negotiating before you know the market.

How Lecong actually works

Lecong is a long strip of large showroom buildings, each housing many brands. It is closer to a permanent furniture exhibition than to a traditional market.

What that means:

  • Showrooms are sales floors, not factories. Production happens elsewhere.
  • Model numbers matter. The same sofa appears under different names in different showrooms.
  • MOQ is per model and per finish. Ask both, separately.
  • Prices are quoted in different Incoterms. Ex-works, FOB and CIF quotes are not comparable.
  • Lead times vary hugely between stock items and made-to-order pieces.

What to compare on furniture

Beyond the headline price:

  • Materials: solid wood species, engineered board grade, foam density, fabric composition and rub count, leather type.
  • Hardware: drawer runners, hinges, fittings — the most common failure point.
  • Finish: lacquer type, edge banding, colour consistency.
  • Construction: joinery method, corner blocks, webbing, leg attachment.
  • Packaging: carton grade, corner protection, foam, whether assembly is required.
  • Loading: carton dimensions and units per container — this often decides profitability more than unit price.
  • Compliance: formaldehyde or emissions standards, fire retardancy, and any labelling your market requires.

Ask for a specification sheet, not a brochure. If a supplier cannot provide measurements and materials in writing, the order will be a negotiation after arrival.

Ceramics and building materials

For tiles, sanitaryware and related products, the comparison points shift:

  • Size tolerance and flatness — critical for installation.
  • Water absorption rate and frost resistance for outdoor use.
  • Slip resistance ratings for flooring in commercial projects.
  • Shade variation between batches, and how the factory controls it.
  • Packing: tiles are heavy and breakable; palletisation and edge protection matter.
  • Container weight limits — tiles hit weight limits before volume limits.

Ask for test certificates referenced to the standard your market uses, and check that the certificate matches the exact product and size.

Freight decides the deal

For large goods, freight is not an afterthought. It frequently outweighs the price difference between two suppliers.

Calculate before you commit:

  1. Carton dimensions and weight per unit — get these in writing.
  2. Units per container at your chosen container size.
  3. Freight cost per container for your route, at current rates.
  4. Insurance for the shipment value.
  5. Duty and taxes in your market — you must supply your own rate.
  6. Inland transport at both ends.

The sourcing cost calculator carries these inputs through to a per-unit landed cost.

Damaged goods: plan for it

Furniture and ceramics arrive damaged more often than small commodities. Reduce the risk:

  • Specify packaging explicitly in the purchase order.
  • Photograph packing during production if you have an inspection.
  • Insure the shipment and understand the claims process before you ship.
  • Keep the approved sample or finish swatch for disputes.
  • Agree a remedy in writing — replacement, credit or refund — before the order ships.

Seeing production

A factory visit is more valuable here than in most categories, because construction quality is hard to judge from a showroom sample.

What to look at:

  • Machinery and tooling — is the factory equipped for the joinery or pressing it claims?
  • Material storage — humidity control matters for wood.
  • Finishing line — dusty or clean?
  • Quality control station — does one exist?
  • Packing area — how are corners protected?

Bring the factory visit checklist and take notes on the spot.

Getting there and staying

Foshan is connected to Guangzhou by metro and rail, with Lecong a further ride from the centre. Many buyers base themselves in Guangzhou and commute, which works if you allow for traffic.

If you are visiting multiple factories, staying in Foshan for two nights is usually more efficient than commuting twice.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing FOB and ex-works prices as if they were the same.
  • Ignoring carton dimensions until the container will not fit the order.
  • Assuming a showroom is a factory.
  • Skipping the packaging specification and paying for it in damage claims.
  • Buying a large order without a pre-shipment inspection.
  • Scheduling Lecong and a ceramics centre on the same morning.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I need in Lecong?

Two full days minimum for furniture, and three if you are buying across several rooms or product types. The showroom area is very large and spread along a main road.

Can I order a single set for a project?

Often yes, but expect project pricing rather than container pricing, and confirm the packing method — single sets are more vulnerable to transit damage.

Do I need an inspection for furniture?

For container orders, a pre-shipment inspection is worth considering, particularly for finish, hardware and packing. It is not a substitute for specifying those things in writing.

Is Foshan only furniture?

No. Ceramics, tiles, sanitaryware and other building materials are significant clusters, usually in different areas from the furniture showrooms.
