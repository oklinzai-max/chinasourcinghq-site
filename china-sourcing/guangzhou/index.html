Guangzhou: Markets & Sourcing Guide

A sourcing guide to Guangzhou beyond the Canton Fair: named wholesale markets by category, which metro station serves each, opening patterns, and how to choose between the fair and the markets.

Quick answer

Guangzhou is China's most useful single city for buyers with several unrelated categories. The Canton Fair covers factories; the permanent markets cover apparel, shoes, bags, fabrics and small goods year-round. Work by district and by named building — Shisanhang and Shahe for volume clothing, Baima and Zhanxi for better apparel and shoes, Guihuanggang for bags, Zhongda for fabric.

Who this guide is for: Buyers sourcing consumer goods across several categories, and first-time visitors who want markets before or after a fair phase.

Two different Guangzhous

Buyers often treat Guangzhou as “the Canton Fair city”. It is also one of China’s largest permanent wholesale centres, with market clusters that operate all year.

Those are two different sourcing environments, and choosing the wrong one wastes the trip:

What you need Canton Fair Permanent markets
A custom product to your design Yes — talk to factories No — finished goods only
Certification and test reports Possible, ask at the booth Rare, and hard to verify
A small trial order this week No Yes, often 5–10 pieces per style
Price signals across many suppliers Yes, dozens in one building Yes, within a cluster
Factory relationship and tooling Yes No
Speed of access Book flights, pick a phase Walk in any weekday

The fair is better for specifications, customisation and factory relationships. The markets are better for speed, breadth and small trial orders.

The named markets, by category

Guangzhou’s markets are grouped by trade, and the groupings sit in different districts. The table below names the buildings buyers actually use, with the nearest metro station. Hours are typical, not guaranteed — several markets open at dawn and close in the early afternoon, and individual buildings keep their own weekly closing day.

Market What it sells Area Nearest metro Typical hours
Shisanhang (New China Building) Fast-fashion womenswear, very high stall density Renmin South Road, Liwan Line 6, Cultural Park, Exit A (about 500 m walk) Early morning to early afternoon
Shahe garment market group Low-price, high-volume clothing Shahe Street, Tianhe Line 6, Shaheding, Exit B Morning to late afternoon
Baima garment market Mid-to-high-end womenswear and menswear Zhannan Road, Yuexiu (by Guangzhou railway station) Line 2/5, Guangzhou Railway Station, Exit G Morning to early evening
Zhanxi road shoe market group Women’s and men’s shoes, shoe materials; includes the Buyun Tiandi building Zhanxi Road, Yuexiu Line 2/5, Guangzhou Railway Station Morning is best for samples
Guihuanggang leather goods Bags, luggage, leather goods Jiefang North Road, Baiyun Line 2, Sanyuanli Mid-morning to evening
Zhongda fabric market Fabrics and trims Ruikang Road, Haizhu Line 8, Zhongda Daytime
Yide Road Stationery, toys, small goods, dried seafood Yide Road, Yuexiu Line 6, Yide Road Daytime

Practical realities:

  • Opening hours are trade hours. Garment markets start early and wind down in the afternoon; arriving at 15:00 is often too late.
  • Some markets close on a fixed weekday. This is the single most common wasted trip.
  • Stalls are wholesale-first. Retail behaviour may be tolerated, not welcomed.
  • The metro exit matters more than the station. Several of these buildings are a 5–15 minute walk from the nearest exit.

Which market for which job

Your situation Go here
Small runs, many styles, stock today Shisanhang or Shahe
Better finishing, private label, brand-ready Baima, and brand stalls along Zhanxi
Shoes and shoe materials Zhanxi road group
Bags and luggage Guihuanggang
Developing your own garment, need fabric Zhongda
Stationery, toys, small gift items Yide Road
Factories, custom tooling, certification Canton Fair, not the markets

A common mistake is treating Shisanhang and Baima as interchangeable. They are not: Shisanhang competes on speed and price, Baima on finish and brand presentation.

Planning market days without wasting them

Cluster by district. Crossing Guangzhou in traffic can consume an hour each way. Two clusters in one day is usually the maximum — Liwan and Yuexiu sit together, Haizhu and Tianhe do not.

Start with one cluster in the morning. Stall owners are more available before the afternoon rush, and you get better attention.

Photograph everything with context. Product, price tag, stall number and business card in one frame. A photo of a bag with no stall number is a souvenir, not a sourcing lead.

Ask about minimums per colour, not per item. “How many pieces per colour?” reveals the real order structure. Use the supplier comparison sheet to keep quotes comparable.

Collect, then compare. Do not negotiate hard at the first stall. Collect five quotes for the same specification, then return to the best two.

One day or three days

If you have one day: pick a single district and do it properly. Liwan + Yuexiu in one day is realistic — Shisanhang at dawn, then Baima or Zhanxi by metro in the afternoon. Do not attempt Haizhu as well.

If you have three days: Day 1 garment volume (Shisanhang, Shahe). Day 2 better apparel and shoes (Baima, Zhanxi, Guihuanggang). Day 3 fabric and small goods (Zhongda, Yide Road), leaving the evening for follow-up messages. This also leaves room to revisit two or three finalists.

Where to stay for a market-focused trip

The fair-area hotels in Pazhou are convenient for the venue but poorly placed for markets in Liwan, Yuexiu and Haizhu’s western side.

For market days, consider:

  • Liwan / Yuexiu — close to several market clusters, more local restaurants.
  • Haizhu (outside Pazhou) — a compromise between markets and venue.
  • Tianhe — comfortable, business-oriented, but you will commute to most markets.

Read the Canton Fair accommodation guide for the fair-side comparison.

What to prepare before you go

  • Product specifications in writing, with dimensions, materials and target price.
  • Photos of reference products, including detail shots of stitching, hardware and labels.
  • A buying plan: which categories, how many SKUs, target order size.
  • Payment setup — see payment and connectivity.
  • WeChat installed and working before you land.

Quality and compliance

Guangzhou markets are fast and commercial; they are not a compliance checkpoint. For anything regulated:

  • Ask for test reports and check which standard they reference.
  • Confirm the report matches the exact product, not a similar one.
  • Use a third-party inspection for production orders above a value you are uncomfortable losing.

We do not recommend specific inspection companies, because we have not vetted any. Any guide that names “the best” without disclosing a commercial relationship deserves scepticism.

Combining Guangzhou with other cities

  • Foshan for furniture and building materials — a short rail or metro hop.
  • Zhongshan for lighting — reachable by rail or road.
  • Shenzhen for electronics — frequent high-speed trains.
  • Yiwu for small commodities — a long leg, best planned as a separate phase of the trip.

The China sourcing itinerary puts these together with realistic day counts.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming markets and the fair are the same trip. They need different planning and often different hotels.
  • Arriving at a garment market after 15:00, when the stalls are packing up.
  • Paying retail prices because you did not ask about wholesale terms.
  • Buying without a written specification, then discovering the bulk order differs.
  • Trying to cover four clusters in one day — two is the realistic maximum.

Frequently asked questions

Are Guangzhou's wholesale markets open during the Canton Fair?

Generally yes, but some operators adjust hours during fair periods and individual stalls may close. Confirm before travelling across the city.

Do I need the Canton Fair to buy in Guangzhou?

No. The permanent markets operate year-round and are the reason many buyers return outside fair dates, when hotels are cheaper and the city is calmer.

How many days do I need for markets?

Two to three days for two or three categories. Each market cluster can absorb a half-day on its own once you include travel.

Which market should I go to first?

Start with the one that matches your largest spend, not the most famous. For volume clothing that is Shisanhang or Shahe; for better womenswear, Baima; for shoes, Zhanxi; for bags, Guihuanggang; for fabric, Zhongda. They are in different districts, so crossing between them costs an hour.

Is English widely spoken in the markets?

More than in most Chinese wholesale markets, but not everywhere. Translation apps and written specifications are still useful.
