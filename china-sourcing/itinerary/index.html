China Sourcing Itinerary: 8–15 Day Routes

Three China sourcing itineraries with day-by-day plans: Canton Fair plus Yiwu, Guangzhou with Zhongshan and Shenzhen, and a wider 10–15 day route covering Foshan and Yiwu.

Quick answer

Pick a route by what you buy, not by how many cities sound impressive. Eight to ten days supports two cities well; ten to fifteen days supports three. Every route below includes arrival and departure buffers, because a sourcing trip that ends on a delayed train ends badly.

Shortest sensible trip
5–6 days, one city
Two-city trip
8–10 days
Wider trip
10–15 days, three cities
Non-negotiable
One buffer day before your flight home

Who this guide is for: Buyers planning a multi-city trip who need realistic day counts and a sensible city order.

Route selection in one minute

If you buy… Use this route Days
Factory-made goods plus small commodities Route A: Canton Fair + Yiwu 10–13
Electronics and general consumer goods Route B: Guangzhou + Zhongshan + Shenzhen 8–10
A wide mix across categories Route C: Guangzhou + Foshan + Shenzhen + Yiwu 12–15
Only small commodities Skip to Yiwu and stay there 4–6

Route A: Canton Fair + Yiwu

10–13 days. Best for buyers who need both factory quotes and small-commodity breadth.

The full day-by-day version lives in the Canton Fair + Yiwu itinerary. The shape:

Days Where What
1 Guangzhou Arrive, badge, orient
2–6 Guangzhou Five-day fair phase, with one day for a factory visit
7 Travel High-speed rail or flight to Yiwu
8–10 Yiwu Market days by district
11–12 Yiwu / departure city Samples, buffer, departure

Why this order: fair dates are fixed and Yiwu is flexible. Starting with the fixed commitment removes the risk of missing it.

Route B: Guangzhou + Zhongshan + Shenzhen

8–10 days. Best for electronics, lighting and general consumer goods in one region.

All three cities sit in the Pearl River Delta, connected by frequent high-speed rail. This is the most efficient multi-city route in China for consumer-product buyers.

Day Where Focus
1 Guangzhou Arrive, metro card, market orientation in the afternoon
2 Guangzhou Wholesale markets relevant to your category
3 Guangzhou Second market cluster or supplier meetings
4 Zhongshan Morning train, Guzhen lighting showrooms in the afternoon
5 Zhongshan Lighting shortlist, component questions, factory visit if arranged
6 Shenzhen Train to Shenzhen, Huaqiangbei in the afternoon
7 Shenzhen Component sourcing, supplier meetings, sample purchase
8 Shenzhen Factory visit or second market pass
9 Shenzhen Buffer, sample shipping, departure prep
10 Depart Fly home from Shenzhen

Adjustments:

  • Drop Zhongshan if lighting is irrelevant; add a second Shenzhen day instead.
  • Add Foshan for furniture and building materials, but accept a longer trip.
  • Reverse the order if you fly into Shenzhen — starting there and ending in Guangzhou works equally well.

Route C: China Sourcing 10–15 Day Route

12–15 days. Best for buyers with several unrelated categories and a tolerance for movement.

Days Where Focus
1 Guangzhou Arrive, orient
2–3 Guangzhou Wholesale markets and supplier meetings
4 Foshan Lecong furniture market
5 Foshan Building materials, ceramics, shortlist
6 Shenzhen Train, Huaqiangbei
7 Shenzhen Components and factory meetings
8 Travel Fly or rail to Yiwu (long travel day)
9–11 Yiwu Market days by district
12 Yiwu Samples, negotiation, consolidation
13–14 Departure city Buffer and departure

This route is demanding. Two rules keep it workable:

  1. Do not schedule negotiations on travel days. You will be tired and rushed.
  2. Ship samples as you go, not at the end. Carrying four cities’ worth of samples is a logistical problem you can avoid.

The rules that make any itinerary survive contact with reality

Rule 1: One buffer day before departure. Trains are late, couriers are slower than promised, and factory visits overrun. A buffer is not wasted time; it is insurance.

Rule 2: Never change cities on the morning of a meeting. Travel in the evening or the previous day.

Rule 3: Cluster by geography, not by importance. Moving between cities costs half a day each time.

Rule 4: One rest half-day per five days. Decision quality collapses without it. A morning off produces better afternoon negotiations.

Rule 5: Book refundable where possible. Factory schedules move. A non-refundable hotel can trap you in the wrong city.

Transport between the cities

  • Guangzhou ↔ Shenzhen: high-speed rail, short journey, very frequent. The easiest leg in China.
  • Guangzhou ↔ Zhongshan: rail or road; allow for traffic if going by car.
  • Guangzhou ↔ Foshan: metro line and rail connections; Lecong needs a further taxi or metro ride.
  • Guangzhou/Shenzhen ↔ Yiwu: a long leg. See the Guangzhou to Yiwu route guide.

We do not publish departure times or fares. Check 12306 or an airline app for your dates — schedules change and a copied timetable is a liability.

Where to stay in each city

  • Guangzhou — Pazhou for the fair, Line 8 corridor for value. See Canton Fair accommodation.
  • Yiwu — near the district you will work. See Yiwu arrival and hotels.
  • Shenzhen — near a metro line with a direct connection to your market or factory area.
  • Foshan — near Lecong if furniture is the priority, otherwise central Foshan near the metro.
  • Zhongshan — Guzhen itself, to avoid a daily commute.

Common mistakes

  • Planning to “decide when I get there”. Cities are big; improvisation costs days.
  • Scheduling a factory visit on departure day.
  • Booking the cheapest hotel far from the market and losing two hours a day.
  • Underestimating sample logistics in a multi-city trip.
  • Forgetting that markets close on public holidays.

Before you book

China high-speed rail between sourcing cities

  • The passport number on the ticket must match the travel document you carry.
  • Second class is usually fine for 1–3 hour journeys; book earlier for long routes.
  • Leave a buffer between a flight arrival and a same-day train.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best first China sourcing itinerary?

For most first-time buyers, Guangzhou plus one product cluster — Shenzhen for electronics, Foshan for furniture, or Zhongshan for lighting — is the most useful introduction. It keeps you in one region, uses short high-speed rail hops, and leaves time for actual conversations.

How many cities is too many?

Three cities in seven days is too many. Movement consumes half-days: packing, checking out, transferring, checking in, orienting. Three cities in twelve days is workable.

Should I plan around the Canton Fair?

Only if the fair covers your categories. Outside fair dates, Guangzhou's permanent wholesale markets are still open and often easier to work without the crowds.

Where should I start if I arrive in Shanghai?

Either take high-speed rail to Yiwu first, or fly to Guangzhou. Starting in Yiwu and finishing in Guangzhou also works, but the fair dates then dictate the tail of your trip.
